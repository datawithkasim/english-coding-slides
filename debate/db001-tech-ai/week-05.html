🗣️SPEAKING
SKILL
Today's skill

Two reasons + signposting

FIRST

Reason one, with its signpost.

First, robots do dangerous jobs, so nobody gets hurt.

SECOND

Reason two, with its signpost.

Second, things cost less, so families pay less.

Every part, every time. Miss one and it is not an argument yet.

SAY IT LIKE THIS
Structure

Two reasons + signposting

The frame
First, . Second, .
First, robots do dangerous jobs, so nobody gets hurt. Second, things cost less, so families pay less.
Not this

"Robots are good because factories are faster and also people don't get hurt and it's cheaper and yeah."

Could you repeat that back? Neither could the judge.

THINK LIKE A DEBATER
🧠 How to break an argument

Three places to attack

1 · The reason
Is that actually why? Could something else explain the same thing?
2 · The example
Is one example enough? Does it even show the thing they said?
3 · The link
Strongest: even if that is true, it does not prove the motion.
Interrupting, legally
Raise a hand: Point of information. They answer Accepted or Not now.
Accepted gives you 15 seconds, and it must be a question. One per speech.
The three that always work: "Says who?" · "So what?" · "Always?"

Never the person. "You do not understand" loses the round on its own.

Where they meet

The two lines that decide it

Answer: For whom? Not the person who was fired.
They say — "It is cheaper"
Answer: Cheaper for the buyer, or for the owner?

Answer with a reason

Then ask: Name the person who lost the old one. What are they doing now?
A machine takes the work that damages backs and lungs. Good news for whom?
Then ask: Good for the worker, or good for the owner? Say which, and how you know.
