Where they meet

The two lines that decide it

They say — "A company can decide faster"
Answer: Faster is not the same as right, and speed is no reason to remove the appeal.
They say — "Somebody must be responsible"
Answer: Name the person. If nobody can be named, nobody is responsible.
Argue it

Take one. Do you believe the answer, or can you beat it?

🧠THINKING
THINK LIKE A DEBATER
🧠 How to break an argument

Three places to attack

1 · The reason
Is that actually why? Could something else explain the same thing?
2 · The example
Is one example enough? Does it even show the thing they said?
3 · The link
Strongest: even if that is true, it does not prove the motion.
Interrupting, legally
Raise a hand: Point of information. They answer Accepted or Not now.
Accepted gives you 15 seconds, and it must be a question. One per speech.
The three that always work: "Says who?" · "So what?" · "Always?"

Never the person. "You do not understand" loses the round on its own.

PREP
🛠️ Build your case

Four things, in this order

  • Argument one — claim, reason, example, and why it matters.
  • Argument two — a different reason. Not the same one again.
  • Their best argument — guess it, write it down, and plan the answer.
  • Two points of information — written as questions, ready to stand up with.
Timekeeper

Call the halfway point out loud, then the last warning.

Before you go

Answer with a reason

Which of their points did nobody answer?
Then ask: Say what you would have said, now that you have had a minute.
Which of your own arguments was the weakest?
Then ask: What would you cut, and what would you put in its place?
What is the best thing the other side said today?
Then ask: Say it as strongly as they did. Then beat it.
