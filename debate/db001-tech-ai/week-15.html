Structure

The closing speech

The frame
Today we showed three things: , , and .
Today we showed three things: … The most important is … For that reason, you should vote for our side.
Not this

"…and also there's another thing, um, so yeah, that's it."

A closing has no new arguments. Remind · weigh · stop.

THINK LIKE A DEBATER
🧠 How to break an argument

Three places to attack

1 · The reason
Is that actually why? Could something else explain the same thing?
2 · The example
Is one example enough? Does it even show the thing they said?
3 · The link
Strongest: even if that is true, it does not prove the motion.
Interrupting, legally
Raise a hand: Point of information. They answer Accepted or Not now.
Accepted gives you 15 seconds, and it must be a question. One per speech.
The three that always work: "Says who?" · "So what?" · "Always?"

Never the person. "You do not understand" loses the round on its own.

LAST QUESTIONS
Before you go

Answer with a reason

Saying "that hurts" is a sentence. What would count as proof?
Then ask: Name the test. No test, no case — that is the whole debate.
Two mistakes: rights for a thing that feels nothing, or none for a thing that suffers. Which is worse?
Then ask: Say why. That is weighing, and weighing is what wins the round.
Rights come with duties. Name one duty a machine could carry.
Then ask: If it cannot carry a duty, does it still get the right?
